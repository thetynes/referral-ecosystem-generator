# Sample ecosystems: commercial-usefulness evaluation

The four requested samples (plus two extra used for the AI comparison) are hypothetical businesses. No real organisations are named. Each complete ecosystem, with every relationship, trigger, decision-maker, first step, and guardrail, is in [`samples/`](samples/):

| Sample | Complete ecosystem | Curated profile used |
|---|---|---|
| Excavation & waterproofing contractor, Sarnia | [`excavation-waterproofing-sarnia.md`](samples/excavation-waterproofing-sarnia.md) | Waterproofing + Excavation (blended), 22 relationships |
| Immigration law firm, Toronto | [`immigration-law-toronto.md`](samples/immigration-law-toronto.md) | Immigration law, 16 |
| Property management company, Southwestern Ontario | [`property-management-swo.md`](samples/property-management-swo.md) | Property management, 12 |
| SEO / digital marketing agency, Ontario | [`seo-agency-ontario.md`](samples/seo-agency-ontario.md) | Marketing, SEO & creative, 14 |
| Custom home builder, Lambton (extra) | [`custom-builder-lambton.md`](samples/custom-builder-lambton.md) | Custom home builder, 11 |
| Family law firm, Hamilton (extra) | [`family-law-hamilton.md`](samples/family-law-hamilton.md) | Family law, 11 |

**The test I applied:** would an experienced owner in this industry read the output and find at least two or three relationships they had not already worked, each with a concrete enough mechanism (trigger, decision-maker, first step) to act on this month? A list of related industries fails this test even if every item is correct.

## 1. Excavation & waterproofing contractor (Sarnia)

**Verdict: strong.** This is the clearest demonstration of the product's thesis.

The obvious relationships (builders, GCs, plumbers, landscapers) are present, but they are not what makes the output worth paying for. The useful findings:

| Overlooked relationship | Why it is commercially real | How it becomes a relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Builders' warranty / customer-care departments** | Statutory new-home warranty coverage (Tarion in Ontario) obliges builders to fix basement water penetration in the early years. The builder has the obligation and the budget; what they lack is a crew that responds fast and documents for the warranty file. Spring-thaw claims cluster by subdivision. | Ask for the **warranty manager**, not the sales office. Offer a standard repair scope, a turnaround commitment, and warranty-ready photo reports. Handle one claim and share the report. |
| **Restoration & mould remediation firms** | They dry out the basement but rarely fix the cause, and groundwater seepage is commonly excluded from home insurance, so the homeowner pays for the cause right after an insured clean-up. The restorer is in the house at that moment and wants to avoid callbacks. | Offer a fast assessment for their files plus a one-page "seepage versus covered water" explainer they can hand to homeowners. |
| **Basement finishing contractors** | Every basement renovation passes a moisture checkpoint, and renovators do not want to warranty drywall over a leaking wall. | A 48-hour assessment for jobs in progress, with a moisture report they attach to their contract. |
| **Landlords converting basements to rental units** | Additional-unit rules are driving conversions; one investor brings several properties. | Speak at investor meetups about the true cost of moisture in rental basements; offer portfolio pricing. |
| **Eavestrough and grading contractors** (counter-intuitive) | Recommending the cheap fix first builds the trust that makes inspectors and insurers keep referring. | A reciprocal "start with the outside" checklist. |

**Weak spots:** Septic designers appear because "Lambton County" implies rural work. That is right for Lambton, but it should be confirmed with the owner. Radon is low priority and correctly ranked last.

## 2. Immigration law firm (Toronto)

**Verdict: strong on recurring corporate channels and legal intersections; regulatory framing is a differentiator.**

| Overlooked relationship | Why it is commercially real | How it becomes a relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Healthcare employers recruiting internationally** | Hospitals and long-term-care homes recruit internationally educated nurses in cohorts, and work-permit timing must align with licensing. One employer can mean many files a year. | Brief the talent-acquisition lead on aligning permits with licensing steps, then propose a cohort service model. |
| **Agriculture, trucking, food-processing employers** | Seasonal, predictable labour-market and permit applications every year. Compliance errors are expensive. | Offer an annual compliance calendar and application service. |
| **Criminal defence lawyers** | A plea or sentence can remove a permanent resident's appeal rights (in Canada, sentences of 6 months or more). Defence counsel need fast immigration advice before resolving cases. | A short continuing-professional-development session for defence lawyers on the immigration consequences of pleas, plus a rapid-consult line. |
| **Licensed consultants (RCICs)** | Consultants cannot appear in Federal Court, and judicial-review deadlines are short. Every refusal they cannot litigate is an urgent, well-defined handoff. | A written protocol on how clients move between practices and back. |
| **Certified translators** | They meet applicants at the very start, before representation is chosen, and cannot advise. | Non-exclusive mutual recommendation with a few reliable translators. |

**Regulatory value:** every legal relationship carries the law-society referral-fee caution, and the profile warns against unlicensed "agents". For lawyers this is not a footnote; it is part of whether the advice is usable.

**Gaps surfaced by the AI comparison (worth vetting for the curated profile):** co-op and work-integrated-learning offices, and employment lawyers for workers on closed work permits after termination. The AI also proposed medical clinics and language-test centres, but designated physicians and test centres operate under neutrality rules, so they are unlikely referral sources. See §5.

## 3. Property management company (Southwestern Ontario)

**Verdict: the most differentiated output of the four.** Most property-management marketing targets realtors and investor clubs. The ecosystem shows where management contracts actually change hands: at moments of owner pain or transition.

| Overlooked relationship | Why it is commercially real | How it becomes a relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Landlord-tenant paralegals** | Self-managing landlords who end up at the Landlord and Tenant Board often decide afterwards to stop managing themselves. The paralegal hears "how do I stop this happening again?" | Send your own tribunal files to one or two landlord-side paralegals, and ask them to pass your information to owners who want ongoing help. |
| **Your maintenance vendors** | Trades see self-managing landlords struggle (third emergency call, access problems, slow payment) long before those owners call a manager. You can offer trades what they want: steady, paid-on-time work orders. | A vendor programme with clear payment terms, and say explicitly that you welcome introductions. Disclose any vendor arrangements to owners. |
| **Estate lawyers and executors** | Estates hold tenanted or vacant homes during administration. Executors often live elsewhere and have fiduciary duties, and vacant-home insurance often requires regular inspections. | An "estate property" service (inspections, rent collection, sale preparation) introduced to estate lawyers. |
| **Accountants for real-estate investors** | They see disorganised rental schedules and losses at tax time, a sign of an owner who is struggling. | Show your owner-statement and year-end package; clean records make the accountant's job easier. |
| **Builders of small rental projects and additional units** | New multiplexes and suites are often owned by first-time landlords. | A lease-up package the builder can present to clients. |
| **Rental-licensing & property-standards compliance** (found by the AI run, then vetted and added) | Several Southwestern Ontario rental markets license rental units. A deficiency notice or property-standards order is a deadline-driven trigger. | Learn each municipality's licensing rules; offer compliance consultants a "licensed and maintained" management package. |

**Guardrail that matters:** Ontario leasing-licensing and condo-management licensing, referral-fee rules for real estate registrants, and disclosure of vendor rebates. A property manager who pays an unlicensed person for referrals takes on real risk; the output says so.

## 4. SEO / digital marketing agency

**Verdict: good, but the weakest of the four.** Agencies are already flooded with partnership advice, so the bar for "non-obvious" is higher.

| Overlooked relationship | Why it is commercially real | How it becomes a relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **Trademark lawyers (forced rebrands)** | A trademark dispute or failed clearance forces a rename and domain migration, the moment when businesses most often lose organic traffic. The work is urgent and budgeted. | Publish a rebrand-migration checklist for IP lawyers and offer a free migration-risk review for their clients. |
| **IT providers and hosts during platform migrations** | They handle the technical move but not redirects or search preservation, and they know about migrations months ahead. | A fixed-price migration-protection package for IT firms. |
| **Recruiters filling marketing roles** | Stalled marketing-manager searches leave work undone, so an agency covers the gap and refers in-house hiring back. | A reciprocal arrangement with a marketing-focused recruiter. |
| **Commercial leasing agents and plaza landlords** | Every new lease is a new location that needs local visibility before opening, and landlords want tenants to succeed. | A new-location launch package introduced to leasing agents. |

**Weak spots:** photographers, PR firms, printers, and niche associations are correct but generic. These are relationships most agencies already know. The value is concentrated in the four rows above.

## 5. AI pipeline versus curated profiles (live OpenAI runs)

Six live runs, OpenAI `gpt-5.5`, reasoning effort `medium`, batches of 4 categories in parallel. Total spend **$3.42** (35k input / 108k output tokens). Raw outputs: `samples/*.ai.json` and `samples/comparison.json`.

| Sample | Latency | Est. cost | Curated / AI relationships | Approx. overlap | Rated "High" (curated → AI) | Relationships only the AI proposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Excavation & waterproofing | 101 s | $0.56 | 22 / 15 | 10 | 13 → 11 | construction associations. Its other "new" items (secondary-suite designers, surveyors, arborists, asphalt) exist in the curated profile but were cut by a blending cap, which is now fixed. |
| Immigration law | 130 s | $0.61 | 16 / 15 | 13 | 10 → 14 | immigration medical clinics, language-test centres, co-op offices |
| Property management | 116 s | $0.56 | 12 / 15 | 11 | 3 → **15** | turnover cleaners, **by-law / rental-licensing compliance** (now added to the curated profile) |
| SEO agency | 124 s | $0.59 | 14 / 15 | 13 | 2 → 9 | M&A advisors, industry newsletters |
| Custom builder | 95 s | $0.55 | 11 / 15 | 11 | 4 → 11 | septic & well specialists (relevant in rural Lambton), demolition contractors, building-supply showrooms |
| Family law | 99 s | $0.55 | 11 / 15 | 13 | 5 → 13 | insolvency trustees (debt in separation), newcomer support centres |

Across all six, the AI rated **81%** of relationships "High", compared with **43%** for the curated engine.

**What the AI adds**
- Business-specific wording for opportunities and triggers. It reads more tailored than the curated text, which is written for the industry.
- Some new relationships worth adding to the curated profiles after vetting. Examples: municipal rental-licensing compliance (property management, **already added** to the curated profile), insolvency trustees (family law), septic and well specialists (rural builders), and co-op offices (immigration).

**Where the AI is weaker**
- **It mostly reproduces the curated relationships.** The pipeline gives it the curated category names as a reference list, so overlap is high. The most valuable overlooked relationships were found by curation, not by AI: builders' warranty departments, municipal flood programmes, LTB paralegals, and trademark-driven rebrands.
- **Score inflation:** the AI rates most relationships "High", which weakens prioritisation. Curated scores spread more.
- **Generic decision-makers** ("Managing Partner", "Director of HR"). The curated profiles name the role that actually decides the referral, such as the builder's *warranty manager* rather than the owner.
- **Boilerplate regulatory notes** repeated on every legal relationship, while missing some specific risks. For example, it proposed language-test centres and immigration medical clinics without noting that designated physicians and test centres are bound by neutrality rules.
- **Latency of about 100–150 seconds per analysis and about $0.55–0.65 per run** with gpt-5.5 at medium effort. That is too slow for a free interactive experience and needs a serverless timeout of at least 180 seconds.

**Recommendation:** keep the curated engine as the product for free users and the default for testers. Use AI as (a) an opt-in tester comparison during the beta, and (b) an internal research tool for expanding curated profiles, with every new relationship human-vetted before it ships. Revisit AI-personalised wording on top of curated relationships, which is cheaper and faster, only if testers say the output feels generic.

## 6. Honest assessment: will customers pay?

**What is convincing:** each sample surfaces 3–5 relationships that most businesses in that industry are not working, with a mechanism specific enough to act on (the trigger, the decision-maker, the first step, and why the partner benefits). That is the product's core value, and it holds across four very different industries.

**What is not yet proven:**
1. **Willingness to pay for categories alone.** With company research disabled, the paid report is mostly the full category list plus the action plan. Because the free tier already shows the five strongest relationships, the paid tier must earn its price through the remaining overlooked relationships and the playbook quality. I would not assume $149/$299 holds without named organisations. Consider testing a single tier (for example, $49–$99) during the beta, or attaching a short human review call.
2. **The two paid tiers barely differ without research.** Comprehensive versus Basic is currently a thin distinction. I'd hide the second tier until research is licensed.
3. **Outcome evidence.** Nobody yet knows whether these relationships produce referrals. Ask each tester to pursue two relationships and report back in 30–60 days. That evidence is what will sell the product later.

**What to measure in the beta (all instrumented):**
- Share of generated maps where the tester opens at least 3 relationships (`relationship_explored`). This is the best leading indicator of perceived value.
- Share of opened relationships that are flagged overlooked. If testers mostly open the obvious ones, the differentiation is not landing.
- Report opens and PDF saves per map (`report_requested`, `report_printed`).
- Upgrade-offer views, then checkout starts, then test purchases. Treat test purchases as intent signals, not revenue.
- A 3-question follow-up per tester: Which relationship was new to you? Did you act on it? What would you pay?
