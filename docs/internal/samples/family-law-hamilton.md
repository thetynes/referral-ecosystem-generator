## How each relationship works

### Mortgage brokers (spousal buyouts & refinancing)

*Upstream referral sources · Two-way referrals · recurring · High (83) · often overlooked*

**Opportunity.** When one spouse wants to keep the home, they must refinance to buy the other out, and lenders generally require a signed separation agreement before funding. Brokers meet separating clients early and need the legal side done properly and promptly.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** The broker's file cannot close until the separation agreement exists, so they actively need a responsive family lawyer. Clients also come to lawyers needing financing advice, so referrals run both ways.

**Who decides.** Mortgage broker

**Trigger events**

- **A separating client asks a broker whether they can keep the home.** A separation agreement must be negotiated and signed before refinancing. *Who notices:* Mortgage broker. *Refer when:* At the first financing conversation. *Their gain:* The broker's file can close.
- **Your client needs to know whether they can afford to keep the home.** A financing pre-assessment informs the negotiation. *Who notices:* You. *Refer when:* Early in negotiations. *Their gain:* A qualified financing client for the broker.

**Mutual value.** Brokers close refinances; you receive clients who need agreements. Your clients make better-informed settlement decisions.

**First step.** Meet two or three brokers who handle spousal buyouts and agree on what each needs from the other and on timelines. Co-author a one-page 'keeping the home after separation' explainer.

**Regulatory.** Mortgage brokers are regulated too. Referral relationships for lawyers and paralegals are regulated by the law society. Paying or receiving anything of value from non-licensees for referrals is generally prohibited, and licensee-to-licensee referral fees carry conditions such as client disclosure and limits. Build these relationships on expertise and reciprocity, and verify against current rules.

### Family mediators

*Complementary providers · Two-way referrals · recurring · High (81)*

**Opportunity.** Mediated agreements require independent legal advice (ILA), and mediators recommend lawyers who support settlement.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** ILA is a recurring, well-defined service, and mediators refer to settlement-oriented lawyers.

**Who decides.** Family mediator

**Trigger events**

- **A mediated agreement is drafted.** Each party needs independent legal advice. *Who notices:* Mediator. *Refer when:* Before signing. *Their gain:* Agreements are signed and hold up.

**Mutual value.** Mediators' agreements are finalised; you gain ILA and drafting work. You can refer clients to mediation.

**First step.** Offer fixed-fee ILA with a clear turnaround for mediators' clients.

**First step.** Co-present to accountants on business owners going through separation.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** This is an established, recurring intake channel that many family lawyers never look into. Panel terms vary, so evaluate them carefully.

## Guardrails

- **Referral compensation.** Referral relationships for lawyers and paralegals are regulated by the law society. Paying or receiving anything of value from non-licensees for referrals is generally prohibited, and licensee-to-licensee referral fees carry conditions such as client disclosure and limits. Build these relationships on expertise and reciprocity, and verify against current rules.
- **Partnerships are not paid-referral arrangements.** This analysis recommends building relationships based on mutual value. It does not assume that referral fees, commissions, exclusivity, or endorsements are permitted in your industry. Check professional, regulatory, and contractual rules before compensating anyone for referrals.
- **Outreach in Canada: CASL applies.** Commercial electronic messages generally need express or implied consent, sender identification, and a working unsubscribe mechanism. Implied consent can exist when a business email address is conspicuously published, the message relates to the recipient's role, and they have not said they don't want such messages. Keep records of how consent was obtained. Personal information you collect is also subject to privacy law (e.g. PIPEDA).
- **No automated outreach.** This tool does not contact anyone on your behalf. Every introduction should be personal, relevant to the recipient, and easy to decline.
