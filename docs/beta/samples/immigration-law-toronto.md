## How each relationship works

### Employers & HR departments hiring foreign talent

*Commercial distribution channels · They refer to you · recurring · High (85)*

**Opportunity.** Employers hiring or retaining foreign nationals need work permits, labour-market assessments, permit extensions, and permanent-residence support for key staff.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** One employer relationship can produce many matters over several years, and the employer often pays. Matters recur as permits expire.

**Who decides.** HR manager / talent-acquisition lead

**Trigger events**

- **An offer is extended to a candidate who needs a work permit.** Authorisation and timing must be secured before the start date. *Who notices:* HR manager or talent acquisition lead. *Refer when:* As soon as the hiring decision is made, before the offer date is fixed. *Their gain:* Avoids failed hires and start-date slippage.
- **A valued employee's work permit or post-graduation permit approaches expiry.** Without a new authorisation or a PR pathway, the employee must stop working. *Who notices:* HR or the employee's manager. *Refer when:* 6–12 months before expiry. *Their gain:* Retains staff they have already trained.

**Mutual value.** HR teams gain predictable timelines and compliance confidence; you gain recurring corporate matters.

**First step.** Offer a short briefing for HR teams on permit timelines and employer compliance obligations. Follow up with a simple intake checklist they can use whenever they hire internationally.

### International recruitment & staffing agencies

*Upstream referral sources · They refer to you · recurring · High (83)*

**Opportunity.** Recruiters who place foreign candidates encounter immigration questions before the employer or candidate has engaged counsel.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** Recruiters see demand early and in volume, but placements fail if immigration steps are mishandled.

**Who decides.** Recruitment agency owner or account manager

**Trigger events**

- **A recruiter shortlists a candidate living abroad.** The candidate needs a work authorisation pathway that fits the role. *Who notices:* Recruiter. *Refer when:* At shortlisting, before the offer. *Their gain:* Fewer failed placements and faster fee collection.

**Mutual value.** Recruiters protect their placement fees; you gain matters from employers and candidates.

**First step.** Work only with recruiters who are properly licensed where required. In Ontario, recruiters of foreign nationals must hold a licence. Offer recruiters a quick eligibility pre-screen process they can explain to clients.

**Regulatory.** Recruiters must not charge prohibited fees to foreign workers. Avoid any arrangement where you pay for referrals. Lawyers' referral relationships are regulated. Most jurisdictions prohibit paying or receiving anything of value from non-lawyers for referrals. Where licensee-to-licensee referral fees are allowed, they come with conditions such as client disclosure and fee limits. Build these relationships on reciprocity and expertise. Verify against your law society or bar's current rules.

### Licensed immigration consultants (RCICs)

*Complementary providers · Two-way referrals · recurring · High (82)*

**Opportunity.** Consultants handle applications but cannot represent clients in Federal Court. Refusals that need judicial review, complex inadmissibility, and litigation are natural handoffs to a lawyer.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** Consultants see many refusals first, and judicial review deadlines are short (often 15 days for decisions made in Canada). That creates urgent, well-defined referrals.

**Who decides.** Licensed immigration consultant (RCIC)

**Trigger events**

- **A client's application is refused.** Judicial review may be the only remedy, and the filing deadline is short. *Who notices:* Consultant. *Refer when:* Immediately after the refusal. *Their gain:* They keep the client relationship while the litigation is handled.

**Mutual value.** Consultants can offer clients a litigation pathway without losing them; you gain urgent litigation matters. You can refer straightforward application work back.

**First step.** Agree in writing how clients move between practices and back. Partner only with consultants in good standing with the regulator.

**Regulatory.** Consultants are regulated by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants. Lawyers' referral relationships are regulated. Most jurisdictions prohibit paying or receiving anything of value from non-lawyers for referrals. Where licensee-to-licensee referral fees are allowed, they come with conditions such as client disclosure and fee limits. Build these relationships on reciprocity and expertise. Verify against your law society or bar's current rules.

### Criminal defence lawyers

*Complementary providers · Two-way referrals · occasional · High (79) · often overlooked*

**Opportunity.** When a non-citizen is charged, plea and sentencing decisions can have severe immigration consequences. Defence counsel need immigration advice before resolving the case.

**Why it makes commercial sense.** This is a high-stakes, time-sensitive intersection. In Canada, for example, a sentence of six months or more can remove a permanent resident's right to appeal removal. Defence lawyers who understand this need an immigration lawyer they can call quickly.

**Who decides.** Criminal defence lawyer

**Trigger events**

- **A non-citizen client is charged with an offence.** The outcome may trigger inadmissibility or removal. *Who notices:* Defence lawyer. *Refer when:* Before any plea or joint sentencing submission. *Their gain:* Protects the client and reduces risk of a later plea challenge.
- **You learn an immigration client has pending charges.** The criminal matter must be handled with immigration consequences in mind. *Who notices:* You. *Refer when:* Immediately. *Their gain:* A well-briefed criminal referral.

**Mutual value.** Defence counsel get fast, specific immigration advice; you get urgent, high-value matters. Referrals flow both ways.

**First step.** Offer a concise CPD-style session for defence lawyers on immigration consequences of pleas, and a fast consultation line for urgent questions.

**Regulatory.** Lawyers' referral relationships are regulated. Most jurisdictions prohibit paying or receiving anything of value from non-lawyers for referrals. Where licensee-to-licensee referral fees are allowed, they come with conditions such as client disclosure and fee limits. Build these relationships on reciprocity and expertise. ## Guardrails

- **Referral compensation.** Lawyers' referral relationships are regulated. Most jurisdictions prohibit paying or receiving anything of value from non-lawyers for referrals. Where licensee-to-licensee referral fees are allowed, they come with conditions such as client disclosure and fee limits. Build these relationships on reciprocity and expertise. Verify against your law society or bar's current rules.
- **Only partner with authorised representatives.** In Canada, only lawyers, Québec notaries, paralegals (in limited contexts), and licensed consultants (RCICs) may represent or advise on immigration matters for a fee. Avoid informal 'agents' or unlicensed intermediaries, because referring to or accepting work through them creates real risk for clients and for you.
- **Partnerships are not paid-referral arrangements.** This analysis recommends building relationships based on mutual value. It does not assume that referral fees, commissions, exclusivity, or endorsements are permitted in your industry. Check professional, regulatory, and contractual rules before compensating anyone for referrals.
- **Outreach in Canada: CASL applies.** Commercial electronic messages generally need express or implied consent, sender identification, and a working unsubscribe mechanism. Implied consent can exist when a business email address is conspicuously published, the message relates to the recipient's role, and they have not said they don't want such messages. Keep records of how consent was obtained. Personal information you collect is also subject to privacy law (e.g. PIPEDA).
- **No automated outreach.** This tool does not contact anyone on your behalf. Every introduction should be personal, relevant to the recipient, and easy to decline.
